Hello there! 👋 Welcome to today's lecture. I'm so excited to guide you through this journey. Today, we are going to dive deep into the Process of Labour.
Imagine childbirth as a huge, universal event—fundamental to human existence—but at the same time, it is a completely unique story for every single woman. 🌍🤰
As nurses, our role is crucial. We are the guardians of safety for both the mother and the baby. From the moment a woman walks into the healthcare agency, we start evaluating her.
💡 Key Tip: It is critical to provide "anticipatory guidance." That's a fancy way of saying: Explain everything before you do it. Tell her what the procedure is (like checking the baby's heart rate), why you're giving medicine, and how it might feel. This lowers her anxiety immensely!
Let's start with assessment
So, how do we check on the mother? 🧐 We use various techniques to see how she is progressing.
First, we look at Vital Signs. This is temperature, blood pressure, pulse, respiration, and pain levels. We also need to peek at her prenatal record to see if there are any risks involved.
Here is the schedule you need to memorize for checking vital signs (blood pressure, pulse, respiration):
🕑 Latent Phase: Every 4 hours.
🕐 Active Phase: Every hour.
⏱️ Transition Phase: Every 15 to 30 minutes.
And for temperature? 🌡️ We check it every 4 hours. However, once the membranes rupture (the water breaks), we must check it every 1 to 2 hours to watch for infection.
Let's move on to the Vaginal Examination
Now, this is a skill that takes time and practice to master. In the US, most nurses in community hospitals do this because doctors aren't always in the room.
Why do we do it? 🤔 We are looking for five main things:
1. Dilation: How open is the cervix?
2. Effacement: How thin is the cervix?
3. Membrane Status: Did the water break?
4. Foetal Position: How is the baby sitting?
5. Station: How far down has the baby dropped?
Before we start, we always explain the procedure to the woman to make her comfortable. She lies on her back. We gently insert the index and middle fingers. ✌️
🚿 Lubrication Rule: If we are checking membranes for the first time, we just use water. If the membranes have already ruptured, we use an antiseptic solution to prevent infection.
Speaking of membranes...
How do we know if the "water broke"? Sometimes it's a gush 🌊, sometimes just a trickle. We use a test called the Nitrazine Test.
Here is the science: Vaginal fluid is acidic, but amniotic fluid (the baby's water) is alkaline.
🧪 The Results:
🟡 Yellow/Olive Green (pH 5-6): Membranes likely intact (it's just vaginal fluid).
🔵 Blue-Green/Deep Blue (pH 6.5-7.5): Membranes likely ruptured (it's amniotic fluid!).
⚠️ Warning: Blood is also alkaline! So if there is a lot of "bloody show," you might get a false positive. If we aren't sure, we do a "Fern Test" under a microscope. Amniotic fluid dries in a pattern that looks like a fern leaf. 🌿
Now let's talk about Power
The primary power of labour is Uterine Contractions. These are involuntary muscles working hard!
Think of a contraction like a wave 🌊. It starts at the top (fundus) and moves down. It has three phases:
1. Increment: Building up.
2. Acme: The peak intensity (the strongest part).
3. Decrement: Letting down.
We measure the intensity (how strong it is) in mmHg.
💪 Mild: feels like the tip of your nose.
💪 Moderate: feels like your chin.
💪 Strong: feels like your forehead (hard!).
We can also monitor contractions electronically at home for high-risk moms. This involves wearing a belt sensor for an hour twice a day. However, interesting fact: Research shows this isn't actually very good at predicting preterm birth, even though we still do it sometimes. 🤷
Let's look at the "Signs of Labour"
How do we know labour is coming? There are "Premonitory Signs" (warning signs) that happen usually 2-3 weeks before term.
⬇️ Lightening: This is when the uterus sinks downward. The baby's head drops into the pelvis.
Good news: Breathing gets easier.
Bad news: Walking gets harder and leg cramps might start due to pressure on the nerves.
🚽 Frequency of Micturition: Because the baby's head is pressing on the bladder, the mom has to pee constantly!
👻 False Pains (Spurious Labour): These are erratic, irregular contractions. They hurt, but they don't actually open the cervix. Usually, walking makes these pains go away!
So, what are the TRUE signs?
True labour means business! 💼 Here is how you tell the difference:
✅ True Contractions: They get stronger, longer, and closer together. Walking makes them worse, not better.
✅ Dilation: The cervix actually opens. This is the most definite sign.
✅ The Show: A blood-stained mucus discharge (the mucus plug comes out).
Now for the Mechanics
This is the physics of birth—the Mechanism of Labour. It's a series of passive movements the baby makes to fit through the birth canal. 🧩
Let's define the key moves:
🔹 Descent: The baby moving down throughout the whole process.
🔹 Flexion: Baby tucks chin to chest (makes the head smaller to fit).
🔹 Internal Rotation: The head twists to fit under the pubic bone.
🔹 Extension: The head is born! It lifts up and out.
🔹 Restitution: The head untwists to align with the shoulders.
🔹 External Rotation: The head turns further as the shoulders rotate inside.
Let's visualize a specific position: LOA
Left Occipito Anterior (LOA) is a very common position. Let's walk through it step-by-step like a dance. 💃
1. Engagement: The baby's head enters the pelvis. The diameter is about 10cm (Sub-Occipito Frontal).
2. Flexion: The uterus pushes down, and the baby tucks its chin. This shrinks the diameter to 9.5cm (Sub-Occipito Bregmatic). Much easier to fit!
3. Internal Rotation: The back of the baby's head (occiput) hits the pelvic floor and rotates 1/8th of a circle forward. Now the head is looking straight down.
4. Crowning & Extension: The head slips under the pubic bone. The face sweeps over the perineum and... the head is born! 🎉
5. Restitution: The neck was twisted, so the head turns back to the left (towards the mother's thigh) to untwist.
6. Shoulder Rotation: Inside, the shoulders twist to fit. This causes the head outside to turn another 1/8th of a circle (External Rotation).
7. Lateral Flexion: The anterior shoulder slips under the pubic bone, the posterior shoulder passes over the perineum, and the whole body slips out sideways. Baby is here! 👶
And that, my friends, is the incredible journey of labour. It is complex, precise, and absolutely amazing.
